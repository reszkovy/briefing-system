2021

QMCD

DiMedical took part in the QCMD 2021 SARS-CoV-2 EQA Programme, confirming the quality of its molecular diagnostics.

About this award

Quality Control for Molecular Diagnostics (QCMD) is an organization that provides an independent international External Quality Assessment (EQA) program. EQA programs play an important role in building effectiveness and increasing employee awareness in monitoring the quality of work in the laboratory. They set the criteria for assessing the ability of a research unit to use molecular biology methods in routine clinical diagnostics. Generating this, the ability to track the direction of changes taking place at individual stages of the diagnostic process. The EQA program primarily provides the laboratory with an independent quality assessment. Thus, it strengthens the quality system implemented in the laboratory and polarizes the performed diagnostic activities towards obtaining reliable test results.

How do EQA programs with QCMD work?

The laboratory receives a series of samples previously characterized as part of the EQA. This is especially true for their evaluation by initial testing to suit the various diagnostic platforms used in laboratories. The analyzed material in the sample represents a wide range of specifications. Thus, representing a complete, clinical picture of the most significant pathogens. This is of fundamental importance in the evaluation of analytical methods used in a laboratory.

2020

Fundament 2020

DiMedical received the Fundament 2020 award, largely for its project on rapid tuberculosis diagnostics.

About this award

It has been known for a long time that the growing epidemic of tuberculosis infections spends sleepless nights not only on dynamically developing research institutions, but also on governments around the world. WHO maintains that every day we are moving away from the postulate to reduce the number of victims to over 80% by 2030. A significant obstacle in the diagnosis of tuberculosis is the time frame needed to confirm the presence of the pathogen in the patient’s body. The only objective evidence of confirming infection with this etiological factor of tuberculosis has been used consistently for several decades by the demanding culture of bacteria. At the same time, revolutionary methods in the field of molecular biology or chromatography play a rather complementary role to the diagnostic test. For this reason, it is so important to search for faster diagnostic methods in the identification of Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
