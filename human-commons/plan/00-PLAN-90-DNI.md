## 0. THE THREE THINGS THE RESEARCH AGREES ON

Before the plan, the three points where all three reports independently land in the same place. Everything below follows from them.

1. **Nobody in the history of this category recruited strangers for cohort one.** Soho House used the restaurant's regulars. Zuzalu used twelve named inviters. Cabin used seven friends. Edge Esmeralda used prior attendees. The projects that tried to recruit strangers (Culdesac, Common, WeLive) had to buy them with discounts and then died. Your recruitment problem is therefore not a marketing problem. It is a problem of assembling six trusted humans who each bring three people.
2. **Every project in the comparable set that owned or long-leased property lost money. Every one that rented per-event did better.** The albergo diffuso model exists precisely to solve your problem: twenty people distributed across restored village houses, one management counterparty, a central reception that forces daily collision, and an owner desperate for shoulder-season nights. This converts your largest capex and your entire regulatory risk into a per-night rate. Do not buy a building.
3. **The site currently converts zero people and contains one claim that is not true.** A `mailto:` twenty screens down, invisible on mobile, pointing at a blank third-party domain, on a page with no human name, no price, no date, and a dated field note describing a scouting trip you have not taken. Every letter you send in the next 90 days ends with someone clicking that page.

Point 3 is why the next 14 days are not about outreach.

Edge City India runs in Goa from 11 October to 1 November. Your scouting trip is planned for October. You cannot do both, and Report A is right that Goa is the densest concentration of qualified candidates that exists — realistically five to ten of your twenty.

### BLOCK 3 — 10 October to 6 November
**Goal: convert the trip into a price, a cohort mechanism, and money in the account.**

1. **Publish the Log entries with real dates, the speed test, and the money.** Both numbers side by side, including the gap. This audience punishes vagueness about money far more harshly than it punishes small numbers. Twenty people at the programme fee is €24,000 and visibly does not restore a village — say where the rest comes from, or say that it doesn't exist yet.
2. **Publish the price, unbundled, on the Vitalia model.** **€1,200 programme fee, four weeks, arrive the same day** — plus your bed passed through at the negotiated albergo diffuso rate (target €900–1,400). Total €2,100–2,600 for a month. Benchmark it openly against Edge Esmeralda (~$2,158/month plus accommodation) and the Monastic Academy AI Fellowship ($3,000/month). The programme fee alone must cover your fixed costs, so that a half-full house is disappointing rather than fatal. Add a **€150 local tier** for residents of the village and the neighbouring comuni: that ten-to-one ratio is not charity, it is your licence to operate, and it is the single clearest thing Edge City got right that Vitalia got wrong.
3. **Appoint six inviters, three seats each.** Named, briefed, from deliberately different disciplines — Zuzalu's four-tribe structure at a tenth of the scale. Application form *after* the invitation, never before. **Do not open a public waitlist for cohort one.** Opaque founder-gatekeeping was Zuzalu's most-cited failure; a delegated invite graph is transparent, produces diversity you would not have chosen, and takes the selection burden off you entirely.
4. **Take deposits: €250, applied against the programme fee, refunded in full only if the residency does not happen.** €2,000 of deposits is not money. It is the only honest measurement you will get before spring, and it costs a person exactly enough to stop them being polite to you.
5. **Announce the cement-mixer date.** March 2027, fifteen to twenty-five candidates at the unfinished site, before anything is ready. That group nominates the twenty. Soho House opened with 500 members because the regulars ran the membership committee seven months before the doors opened; this is the single highest-value mechanic in the entire comparable set and it costs you nothing but a date on a page.
6. **Goa, 19–31 October — conditional on the Block 2 gate.** If Italy delivered, go and talk to people in person with photographs. If it did not, stay home and fix Italy.

**Measurable outcome, Friday 6 November:**
- **8 paid deposits** (€2,000)

**Gate 1 — Friday 4 September. Does anyone answer?**

- **ITERATE** if you have the letters but not the emails: the audience exists, the page still fails. Do not fly until the page converts, because the trip's value is the *content* it produces and content lands on a page.
- **STOP AND RETHINK** if fewer than 20 addresses and zero letters answered by 11 September. That is not a conversion problem. That is the manifesto not landing with the people best equipped to receive it, and no amount of Italian scenery repairs it.

**Gate 2 — Friday 9 October. Is there a real place with a real number?**
- **GO** if you have a named village and one counterparty willing to put twenty beds at a stated rate for spring 2027 in writing. Go to Goa on the 19th. Publish everything.
- **ITERATE** if you have villages you like but no counterparty. Push the residency to autumn 2027 publicly and immediately. Slipping a date honestly costs you nothing; discovering in March that you have no beds costs you the project.
- **STOP** if after thirty villages nobody in three regions will quote a shoulder-season rate for twenty beds. That means the albergo diffuso route does not exist for you, which means you are back to acquiring property, which means a five-year nine-figure-adjacent bet that killed everyone who tried it. **Gate 3 — Friday 6 November. Will anyone pay?**
- **GO** at **8 or more paid deposits and ≥4 named inviters**. This is when the €30–40k becomes a defensible spend. Full commitment: hold the beds, publish the twenty seats, run the March cement-mixer.
- **ITERATE** at **4 to 7 deposits**. The thing is real but the offer is wrong. The two most likely faults, in order: the price is bundled when it should be unbundled, and the afternoons are vague. "Craft" with no published curriculum is the tell that turns your residency into a luxury holiday with a manifesto stapled to it, and this audience finds that tell immediately. Publish the actual afternoon programme, republish, re-ask in January.

## 6. WHAT NOT TO DO

**1. Do not buy, option, or take a long lease on property in 2026 or 2027.** Not a ruin, not a €1 house, not a "we could always resell it." Every project in the comparable set that carried fixed real estate between cohorts lost money — Roam, WeLive, Common, The Assemblage's buildings sold at a 20% loss. Ollolai has sold ten €1 houses in seven years, and the bottleneck was never the price of the house: it was permits, contractors and renovation capital, at timelines measured in years. The moment you sign anything to "keep the place warm between cohorts," you have converted a pre-paid, zero-vacancy, no-lease-liability business into the one that kills 60% of operators within 24 months.

**2. Do not build the Instagram account.** Six posts, one follower. There is a version of the next 90 days in which you post daily, learn Reels, and end November with 900 followers and zero deposits. It will feel like progress every single day and it is the single most seductive way to lose this quarter. Instagram is a place to put the villages while you are in them, once, in October. It is not a channel. Twenty seats get filled by six people making three introductions each, and none of those introductions happen on a grid.

**3. Do not build any governance apparatus.** No Discord, no token, no DAO, no membership constitution, no community platform, no Notion wiki for the residents. Cabin's stated cause of death was that operational overhead exceeded what the network could sustain — the failure was never the cabin, it was everything bolted onto the cabin. One legal entity, one decision-maker, for the first three cohorts. The only governance artefact you need before spring is one page: how people are selected, who arbitrates a conflict, and what the refund terms are.

**4. Do not write more manifesto.** No second essay, no book proposal, no film with a crew, no redesign of a site you redesigned in August. The writing is already better than the substance behind it, and that gap is the thing sceptics detect. From here the only publishable content is evidence: a speed test, a quote, a price, a village with a name, a photograph with you in it. One artefact per month, each one containing something that actually happened.

**5. Do not chase reach before you have a village — and do not open a public waitlist.** Ted Gioia's 500,000 readers are worth nothing to you in September and a great deal in February. A big amplification landing on a page with no place, no price and no cohort converts curiosity into a permanent verdict of "beautiful, vapourware," and you cannot re-launch to the same people. The same logic kills the open application form: Zuzalu's most-cited failure was opaque selection, and the cure is a delegated invite graph, not a public queue you then have to reject people from. Invitations first, form second, and the form is a formality.
