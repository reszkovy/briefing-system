The Ideas

Theurgy

TL;DR

Theurgy (Greek theourgia, "god-working") is late-antique ritual practice aimed at uniting the soul with the divine, defended philosophically by Iamblichus and systematized by Proclus. It works, Iamblichus argued, through divine symbols and divine action — not human thinking. It is a cousin of Hermetism, not its core.

Can ritual do what philosophy cannot? Late antiquity’s most sophisticated answer was theourgia — “god-working” — ritual practice aimed at uniting the soul with the divine.

In the sources

Its great defence is Iamblichus’ Response to Porphyry (transmitted as De mysteriis, c. 300 CE), written under the persona of an Egyptian priest. Against Porphyry’s scepticism, Iamblichus argues that ritual works through divine symbols (synthēmata) and divine action: “it is not thought that links the theurgists to the gods.” A century later Proclus ranked theurgy above philosophy for the soul’s ascent. The Hermetic connection is explicit: the De mysteriis opens by invoking Hermes and claims to answer from “the books of Hermes” — a Neoplatonist philosopher treating Hermetic literature as authoritative Egyptian theology.

The Hermetic relation, kept clean

The philosophical Hermetica are not theurgic manuals; the Corpus Hermeticum teaches through dialogue and vision, not rite. But the Asclepius (23–24, 37–38) — humans “making gods” by drawing spirits into statues — supplied the most famous ancient description of what later readers assimilated to theurgy. Scholars debate the linkage: Bull argues Hermetic ritual and Iamblichus’ defence both grew from Egyptian priestly milieus; others see parallel cousins rather than one lineage. Since Gregory Shaw’s work (1995), theurgy is read as coherent religious philosophy, not Dodds’ “decline into magic”.

Traps

Occultist reception from the Golden Dawn onward uses “theurgy” for ceremonial magic generally and labels the whole package “Hermetic magic”, fusing categories the ancients kept distinct. The tidy scheme “theurgy = white magic, goetia = black magic” is also a later simplification — it is not Iamblichus’ distinction.

Why it matters

Theurgy is where late-antique philosophy admitted that knowing about the divine is not the same as reaching it — the same gap the Hermetic rebirth addresses from another side. Understanding the two as neighbours, not synonyms, is a good test of whether a modern book on “Hermetic magic” knows its history.

Fair questions

Is theurgy a Hermetic practice?

Not strictly. Theurgy belongs to Neoplatonism (Iamblichus, Proclus). The philosophical Hermetica are not theurgic manuals, but the Asclepius' statue-animation passages describe something later readers assimilated to theurgy, and Iamblichus cited 'the books of Hermes' as authority.

Is theurgy just magic?

Iamblichus would say no: theurgy works through divine symbols and divine action, not human technique, and aims at the soul's union with the gods. 'Theurgy = white magic vs. goetia = black magic' is a later simplification.

Did Neoplatonists agree about theurgy?

No — it was a live debate. Plotinus and (ambivalently) Porphyry held philosophy alone leads up; Iamblichus, then Proclus, argued ritual is necessary because thought alone cannot bridge the gap to the gods.
