## 1. FACT — who, when, where

- **Zosimos of Panopolis (Greek Ζώσιμος; Latin Zosimus Alchemista) is the first alchemist in history known by name as a real, datable individual author** — earlier alchemical writings (Pseudo-Democritus, Maria the Jewess, Agathodaimon) are pseudonymous, legendary, or known only through later quotation, largely through Zosimos himself.
  Źródło: Wikipedia, "Zosimos of Panopolis" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Mertens, "Zosimus, alchemist," *Wiley Encyclopedia of Ancient History* — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah21346 (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Floruit: c. 300 CE (late 3rd – early 4th century).** Dating evidence: he cites Julius Africanus (d. after 240 CE); he refers to the Serapeum of Alexandria as still standing (destroyed 391 CE); possible allusions to Manichaeism newly present in Egypt fit the same window. So: a hard *terminus post quem* and *ante quem*, not a birth/death record.
  Źródło: Encyclopedia.com, "Zosimos of Panopolis" (Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography) — https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/zosimos-panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Place: Panopolis in Upper Egypt = modern Akhmim**, a Greco-Egyptian town with a major temple of Min (assimilated to Pan, hence the Greek name) and a documented milieu of Greek-writing Egyptian literati in late antiquity. Zosimos writes in Greek but repeatedly invokes Egyptian temple knowledge, priests, and "the sanctuaries."
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Dufault, "Zosimos Aigyptiakos" (on the Egyptian context of the Visions) — https://www.academia.edu/93234537/Zosimos_Aigyptiakos_Identifying_the_Imagery_of_the_Visions_and_Locating_Zosimos_of_Panopolis_in_His_Egyptian_Context (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **The Suda (10th-c. Byzantine lexicon) credits him with 28 books "designated by letters of the alphabet,"** often identified with the *Cheirokmeta* ("things wrought by hand") — the earliest known titled alchemical book-series. The *Cheirokmeta* as such is lost; what survives are excerpts and treatise-clusters in Byzantine compilations.
  Źródło: Encyclopedia.com — https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/zosimos-panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Addressee: Theosebeia**, a woman to whom nearly everything he wrote is addressed — student, patron, or literary device (see Debate §5.4). The *Final Count* is a letter to a "purple-clad woman" (πορφυρόστολος), standardly identified with her.
  Źródło: search-verified via Fraser, "Baptised in Gnôsis," *Dionysius* 25 — https://ojs.library.dal.ca/dionysius/article/download/dio25fraser/2537 (accessed 2026-08-14); Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).

## 2. FACT — works and what actually survives

- **Four main Greek textual groupings:** (1) *Authentic Memoirs* (Gr. *gnēsia hypomnēmata*) — 13 opuscules incl. *On the Letter Omega* and the three lessons *On Excellence/Virtue* (= the "Visions"); (2) *Chapters to Eusebia*; (3) *Chapters to Theodore*; (4) the *Final Count* (a.k.a. *Final Abstinence*). Titles like *Cheirokmeta* and *Book of Keys* are known but lost as wholes.
  Źródło: Encyclopedia.com — https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/zosimos-panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Mertens edition description via search — https://www.abebooks.com/9782251004488/Alchimistes-grecs-1re-partie-Zosime-2251004483/plp (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Technical content is real chemistry-craft:** multi-piped alembics for distillation, the *kērotakis* (reflux/vapor apparatus for tincturing metals), furnaces, "divine water" (*theion hydōr* — a pun: "divine water" / "sulphur water"). Zosimos also preserves and cites his predecessors: Pseudo-Democritus, Maria the Jewess (to whom the bain-marie is traditionally traced), Agathodaimon.
  Źródło: Encyclopedia.com — https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/zosimos-panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **His definition of the art** (from the Syriac tradition): the study of "the composition of waters, movement, growth, embodying and disembodying, drawing the spirits from bodies and bonding the spirits within bodies."
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).

## 3. FACT — the Visions (the famous dream-sequences)

- **Where they sit:** the three lessons *On Excellence* (Peri aretēs) inside the *Authentic Memoirs* — commonly called "the Visions of Zosimos."
  Źródło: AbeBooks/Mertens edition summary — https://www.abebooks.com/9782251004488/Alchimistes-grecs-1re-partie-Zosime-2251004483/plp (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Core imagery (Lesson I):** the dreamer sees a bowl-shaped altar reached by fifteen steps; a priest, **Ion, "priest of the inner sanctuaries,"** declares he has undergone "unendurable torment": he was dismembered by the sword, flayed, burned, and transformed "from body into spirit." Around the altar men are boiled alive in bubbling water; a series of metallic men appear in the sequence (a brazen man, a leaden man, a man of silver, of gold in later parts), plus the *anthroparion* — a little homunculus-like figure — and a barber-figure connected with flaying. Zosimos wakes repeatedly and interprets: the torments are the operations — the "punishment" of matter is its refinement.
  Źródło: Wikipedia, "Zosimos of Panopolis" (Visions section) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Fraser, "Baptised in Gnôsis" — https://ojs.library.dal.ca/dionysius/article/download/dio25fraser/2537 (accessed 2026-08-14). *Uwaga produkcyjna: przy publikacji cytatów z Ion brać brzmienie z Mertens 1995 (fr.) albo z ang. przekładu w Dufault/Fraser, nie z parafraz internetowych.*
- **Editorial anchor:** critical edition and French translation of the *Authentic Memoirs*: **Michèle Mertens, *Les alchimistes grecs*, t. IV.1: *Zosime de Panopolis, Mémoires authentiques* (Les Belles Lettres, 1995)** — with full manuscript survey, study of the technical apparatus, and notes on the Hermetic-Gnostic background. This is the standard scholarly text of the Visions.
  Źródło: AbeBooks record — https://www.abebooks.com/9782251004488/Alchimistes-grecs-1re-partie-Zosime-2251004483/plp (accessed 2026-08-14); Amazon record of the 2002 reprint — https://www.amazon.com/Alchimistes-grecs-1re-partie-Universitaes/dp/2251004483 (accessed 2026-08-14).

## 4. FACT — Zosimos and the Hermetica

- **Zosimos is one of the earliest external witnesses to Hermetic literature.** In the *Final Count* he tells Theosebeia, once perfected: "spit on matter and, hastening towards Poimenandres and receiving baptism in the mixing-bowl (krater), hasten up towards your own race." This names **Poimandres** (cf. CH I) and the **krater** (cf. CH IV, "The Mixing-Bowl") — evidence that treatises like CH I and CH IV circulated and were read as a devotional path by c. 300 CE. Greek text ed. Festugière, *La Révélation d'Hermès Trismégiste* I (1944), p. 363.
  Źródło: search-verified quotation and Festugière reference via ARYS article "Was Zosimus of Panopolis Christian?" — https://e-revistas.uc3m.es/index.php/ARYS/en/article/view/6795 (accessed 2026-08-14); Fraser — https://ojs.library.dal.ca/dionysius/article/download/dio25fraser/2537 (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **In *On the Letter Omega* he cites "Hermes" against fate/astrology:** the spiritual man who knows himself should not correct anything through magic nor force necessity, but let fate act on the "statue" (the body) — Hermes and Zoroaster are invoked as authorities that the philosophers' race is "above fate." Also here: the fallen-angels motif — the arts (incl. metallurgy) taught to human women by descending angels, echoing the *Book of Enoch* tradition.
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Fraser — https://ojs.library.dal.ca/dionysius/article/download/dio25fraser/2537 (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Consequence for the site's architecture:** Zosimos is the hinge between the "philosophical Hermetica" (CH, Asclepius) and the "technical Hermetica" (alchemy, astrology, magic) — the living proof that around 300 CE one person could hold both as a single curriculum. Cross-link: ideas-glossary.md §9 (philosophical/technical divide).

## 5. FACT — manuscript tradition and translations

- **Greek:** everything Greek descends from a Byzantine anthology of alchemical authors compiled c. 7th–8th century in Constantinople. Three principal witnesses: **Marcianus graecus 299** (Venice, 10th–11th c., the oldest and best, with alchemical sign-lists), **Parisinus graecus 2325** (13th c.), **Parisinus graecus 2327** (15th c., copied 1478).
  Źródło: Encyclopedia.com — https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/zosimos-panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Syriac:** substantial Zosimos material survives in Syriac (Cambridge collection), transmitting works lost in Greek — a still-active edition front (Martelli and collaborators).
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Arabic:** Ibn al-Nadim's *Fihrist* (10th c.) lists four books under garbled forms of his name ("Thosimos," "Dosimos," "Rimos"); Fuat Sezgin located 15 Arabic manuscripts (Tehran, Cairo, Istanbul, Gotha, Dublin, Rampur); in 1995 Zosimos texts were identified inside al-Ṭughrāʾī's *Keys of Mercy*. The Arabic *Book of Pictures* (Muṣḥaf aṣ-ṣuwar, dialogue with Theosebeia in 13 chapters) and *Book of the Keys of the Work* were published in English by **Theodor Abt & Wilferd Madelung** (Living Human Heritage, Zurich). Caveat for the site: the Arabic corpus mixes genuine transmission with later reworking — attribution is contested item by item.
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Modern editions:** **Marcelin Berthelot & C.-É. Ruelle, *Collection des anciens alchimistes grecs* (3 vols., Paris 1887–88)** — first full corpus with French translation, chemically informed but textually unreliable by modern standards; superseded for the *Authentic Memoirs* by **Mertens 1995** (see §3). Berthelot's volumes are public domain and scannable-linkable for the site.
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14); Encyclopedia.com — https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/zosimos-panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).

## 6. SCHOLARLY DEBATE

**6.1 Are the Visions real dreams or literary allegory?**
- *Real visionary/dream experience (at least in form):* **C. G. Jung** treated them as authentic archetypal dream material (see §7); some religious-studies readings keep an experiential core.
- *Deliberate literary allegory of laboratory operations:* **Michèle Mertens** (1995) and most historians of alchemy read the Visions as a carefully composed allegory — the "torments" map onto distillation, calcination, and the coloring sequence of metals; the dream-frame is a genre device (cf. revelation literature). **Olivier Dufault** ("Zosimos Aigyptiakos") pushes further: the imagery is decodable against Egyptian temple ritual and iconography, i.e. cultural encoding, not private psychology.
  Źródła: Mertens edition — https://www.abebooks.com/9782251004488/Alchimistes-grecs-1re-partie-Zosime-2251004483/plp ; Dufault — https://www.academia.edu/93234537/Zosimos_Aigyptiakos_Identifying_the_Imagery_of_the_Visions_and_Locating_Zosimos_of_Panopolis_in_His_Egyptian_Context (both accessed 2026-08-14).

**6.2 "Spiritual alchemy" — how spiritual was it really?**
- *Integrated spiritual-material art:* **Kyle Fraser** ("Baptised in Gnôsis") and **Shannon Grimes** (*Becoming Gold*, 2018) argue Zosimos's chemistry and soteriology form one system: the alchemical vessel doubles as baptismal krater, purification of metals mirrors purification of the soul.
- *Caution against retro-projection:* **Lawrence Principe** (*The Secrets of Alchemy*, 2013) and the Chicago school warn that "spiritual alchemy" as a category owes more to 19th-century occultism and Jung than to the sources; Zosimos is the *one* ancient author where the mystical reading is genuinely grounded — which is exactly why he gets over-used as a proxy for all alchemy.
- *Middle positions:* **Garth Fowden** (*The Egyptian Hermes*, 1986) places Zosimos within Hermetic "technical" literature whose practitioners could ascend to the philosophical Hermetica — the two registers are stages, not opposites.
  Źródła: Fraser — https://ojs.library.dal.ca/dionysius/article/download/dio25fraser/2537 ; Fowden/Bull framing via ARYS — https://e-revistas.uc3m.es/index.php/ARYS/en/article/view/6795 (both accessed 2026-08-14).

**6.3 Was Zosimos a Christian? A Gnostic? A Hermetist?**
- *Christian (or Christian-adjacent):* **Christian H. Bull** (2018; 2020) and **Jourdan & Edwards** (2021) read him as (probably) Christian, using Jewish-Christian apocalyptic (Enoch, "Son of God" language) natively.
- *Gnostic-Hermetic eclectic, not confessionally Christian:* the ARYS study "Was Zosimus of Panopolis Christian?" surveys the evidence and stresses how loose the labels are: Zosimos quotes Hermetica, Nikotheos (a figure also revered in Gnostic circles), Enochic myth, and Egyptian priestly lore in one breath. **Gedaliahu Stroumsa** earlier explored the Gnostic connections.
- The honest summary for the site: Zosimos shows that c. 300 CE Egyptian esoteric religion was a shared reservoir — "Hermetic," "Gnostic," and "Christian" are modern sorting bins he predates.
  Źródła: ARYS — https://e-revistas.uc3m.es/index.php/ARYS/en/article/view/6795 ; ResearchGate mirror — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366402563_Was_Zosimus_of_Panopolis_Christian (both accessed 2026-08-14).

**6.4 Who was Theosebeia?**
- *Historical student/patron:* the mainstream default (Mertens; the Arabic *Book of Pictures* dialogues assume a real interlocutor).
- *Partly literary construct:* the *Final Count* addresses an unnamed "purple-clad woman"; some scholars note the pedagogical-epistolary genre invites a stylized addressee. Either way she is, after Maria the Jewess, the second named woman at the origins of alchemy — worth its own sidebar.
  Źródło: Fraser — https://ojs.library.dal.ca/dionysius/article/download/dio25fraser/2537 (accessed 2026-08-14).

**6.5 How much of the Arabic Zosimos is Zosimos?**
- *Optimists:* **Abt & Madelung** publish the *Book of Pictures* as substantially authentic (with Jungian framing in Abt's commentary).
- *Sceptics:* **Manfred Ullmann** and later philologists treat much Arabic "Zosimos" as pseudepigraphic accretion; each text needs individual proof. For the site: flag the *Book of Pictures* as "transmitted under his name, authenticity debated."
  Źródło: Wikipedia (translations section, citing Sezgin, Ullmann, Abt/Madelung) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).

## 7. RECEPTION & MYTH

- **Jung made Zosimos famous.** C. G. Jung analyzed the Visions from 1937 on ("The Visions of Zosimos," in *Alchemical Studies*, CW 13) as archetypal transformation-dreams — the dismembered priest as the suffering, redeemable spirit in matter. This reading built 20th-century "alchemy as psychology" and is why Zosimos appears in therapy-blogs and popular esoterica far more than any other Greek alchemist. The site should present Jung as *reception*, not as an account of what Zosimos meant.
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis ; example of the popular-Jungian genre: https://gettherapybirmingham.com/blog/zosimos-of-panopolis-the-alchemical-philosopher-and-his-legacy/ (both accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Myth: "Zosimos invented alchemy."** He did not — he is the first *named* author in an already-existing tradition (Pseudo-Democritus c. 1st c. CE, Maria the Jewess) and is himself our main source for those predecessors. First historical alchemist ≠ first alchemist.
- **Myth: "the Visions describe a mystical initiation ritual Zosimos underwent."** No evidence of any ritual; the texts are (at minimum) heavily literary, and the "torments" self-decode as apparatus operations (§6.1).
- **Myth: gold-making charlatan.** Zosimos himself polices this line — he attacks "untimely" tincture-mongers and demonic deception in the arts; his stated goal is knowledge and liberation, with metallic transformation as its exercise. Good inoculation against the "alchemy = greedy proto-fraud" trope.
  Źródło: Fraser — https://ojs.library.dal.ca/dionysius/article/download/dio25fraser/2537 (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Afterlife in Islamicate and Latin alchemy:** cited by Ibn Umayl and the Andalusi corpus (Maslama al-Qurṭubī, cf. emerald-tablet-arabic.md §3.3); imagery of dismemberment/putrefaction-to-perfection resurfaces in Latin emblem alchemy (*Rosarium Philosophorum*, *Mutus Liber*) — a motif chain, not a documented reading-line, so phrase cautiously.
  Źródło: Wikipedia — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zosimos_of_Panopolis (accessed 2026-08-14).
- **Pop-culture surface:** Zosimos circulates today mainly via Jungian blogs, Fandom wikis, and "gnostic mysticism" listicles that fuse fact and reverie — exactly the gap a sourced Figures card can own.
  Źródło (example of the genre): https://www.crazyalchemist.com/mysteries-esoterica/zosimos-of-panopolis-the-pioneer-of-alchemy-and-gnostic-mysticism/ (accessed 2026-08-14).

## 8. CONTENT HOOKS

1. **"The first chemist we know by name had nightmares about it."** Open the Figures card with Ion's torment — dismembered, burned, "from body into spirit" — then reveal: it's (also) a description of a distillation apparatus. Hook = the double decoding.
2. **"Baptized in the mixing-bowl."** The *Final Count* quote sending Theosebeia to Poimandres and the krater is the single best proof-text that the Corpus Hermeticum was *lived*, not just read, c. 300 CE — bridges Path 09 back to Path 04 (Poimandres) and CH IV.
3. **A woman at the beginning of chemistry.** Theosebeia (plus Maria the Jewess, whom Zosimos preserves): alchemy's origin story has named women in it from page one — strong, factual, counter-intuitive angle.
4. **28 books, three languages, mostly rubble.** The transmission story (Suda's 28 books → one Byzantine anthology → Marcianus 299 → Syriac and Arabic lifeboats) as a mini-thriller about how ancient science survives — pairs with the site's manuscript-tradition motif.
5. **"Spiritual alchemy: true here, false everywhere else."** Zosimos is the one ancient author where the mystical reading is textually justified (Fraser, Grimes) — and precisely therefore the favorite proxy of those who project it onto all alchemy (Principe's caution). Perfect "nuance" piece for the brand.
6. **Jung's favorite alchemist.** How a 4th-century Egyptian became a 20th-century psychological icon — reception-history explainer that lets the site cover the Jungian internet without endorsing it.
7. **Above the stars, beside the furnace.** *On the Letter Omega*: Hermes and Zoroaster on the sage "above fate" — Zosimos as living link between philosophical and technical Hermetica, the site's cleanest answer to "were these the same tradition?"
8. **Fallen angels taught us metallurgy.** The Enochic myth inside an alchemical treatise: late-antique Egypt as a shared reservoir where Jewish apocalyptic, Hermetism, Gnosticism, and craft knowledge mixed — sets up the "labels came later" argument (Debate §6.3).
