John Dee

TL;DR

John Dee (1527–1608/9) was an Elizabethan mathematician, geographer and royal adviser who designed a universal symbol — the Monas Hieroglyphica (1564) — and, from 1582, meticulously recorded "angel conversations" conducted with the medium Edward Kelley. He was not a secret agent called 007, and the "Enochian magic system" attributed to him is largely a later construction.

On this page

  2. The angel conversations
  3. How Hermetic was Dee?

Few careers hold together as strangely as John Dee’s: royal adviser and geographer to Elizabethan England, serious mathematician — and, for years, a man who sat beside a scryer writing down what angels allegedly said. The temptation is to split him into a scientist who went wrong. The scholarship says: don’t.

One glyph to hold everything

His Propaedeumata Aphoristica (1558) set out an astral-physical natural philosophy. Six years later the Monas Hieroglyphica (Antwerp, 1564) compressed his ambitions into a single composite glyph of his own devising, expounded in 24 theorems, uniting astronomical, alchemical and Kabbalistic symbolism — a one-character theory of everything, and the fullest expression of his alchemical natural philosophy. Its cast of mind — the cosmos as a web of correspondences a sign can capture — is the world of as above, so below.

The angel conversations

From 1582 Dee held scrying sessions with the medium Edward Kelley, recording thousands of pages of “angelic” dictation in his diaries. Deborah Harkness’s study treats the sessions not as senile credulity but as a coherent program: a universal science sought directly from the source, when books had run out. That a convicted forger sat at the crystal is part of the documented record too — the reality is stranger than any embellishment.

How Hermetic was Dee?

Frances Yates cast him as the archetypal “Hermetic magus” of Elizabethan England. Nicholas Clulee’s John Dee’s Natural Philosophy (1988) took the portrait apart: Dee’s sources were far more eclectic — medieval optics, Roger Bacon, Kabbalah — and his career falls into distinct stages rather than one magus-arc. Dee is a key case study in the collapse of the strong Yates thesis, mapped with the rest of the tradition on the Atlas.

Two myths to retire

Dee was not “the Queen’s secret agent 007” — that tale was popularized by Richard Deacon’s 1968 biography. And the ceremonial “Enochian magic system” sold under his name is largely a later construction, re-engineered from his diaries by the Golden Dawn three centuries on. The documented Dee — a royal mathematician transcribing angelic dictation night after night — needs neither.

Fair questions

Did John Dee really talk to angels?

He believed so, and recorded thousands of pages of scrying sessions held with Edward Kelley from 1582. Harkness reads the diaries as a coherent program of natural philosophy — an attempt at universal science — whatever one makes of the angels.

Was John Dee a spy called 007?

No. The "Queen's secret agent 007" story is a modern embellishment popularized by Richard Deacon's 1968 biography, not a documented fact.

What is the Monas Hieroglyphica?

A 1564 treatise expounding, in 24 theorems, a single composite glyph of Dee's own devising that unites astronomical, alchemical and Kabbalistic symbolism — his one-symbol summary of the cosmos.
