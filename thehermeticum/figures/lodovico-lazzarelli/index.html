Lodovico Lazzarelli

TL;DR

Lodovico Lazzarelli (1447–1500), an Italian poet-humanist, translated the three treatises missing from Ficino's Corpus Hermeticum and — uniquely among Renaissance figures — actually practised Hermetic rebirth as a Christian spirituality. Wouter Hanegraaff calls him "the real Hermetic tradition" of the Renaissance; almost nobody has heard of him.

On this page
  1. Finishing Ficino’s book
  2. The prophet on horseback
  3. The Crater Hermetis

Every history of Renaissance Hermeticism runs Ficino–Pico–Bruno. The figure who arguably deserves the headline is missing from most of them. Lodovico Lazzarelli did two things nobody else did: he completed the Latin Corpus Hermeticum, and he lived it.

Finishing Ficino’s book

Ficino’s celebrated 1463 translation covered only the fourteen treatises in Cosimo’s manuscript. Around 1482 Lazzarelli translated the final three (CH XVI–XVIII, the Diffinitiones Asclepii) from a manuscript Ficino never saw — quietly completing the Latin corpus that Renaissance Europe read. That alone should earn him a footnote in every account; what follows earns him this card.

The prophet on horseback

In 1484 a wandering preacher named Giovanni “Mercurio” da Correggio rode into Rome dressed as an apocalyptic Hermes-Christ figure. Where others saw street theatre, Lazzarelli saw a reborn Hermes Trismegistus — and converted. He styled himself a new Enoch (his Epistola Enoch describes the scene) and reoriented his life around what he had witnessed. It is one of the strangest documented meetings of the Renaissance: the tradition’s best philologist kneeling to its wildest performer.

The Crater Hermetis

His dialogue Crater Hermetis (c. 1493) — the title echoes the mixing-bowl of Corpus Hermeticum IV — fuses Hermetic rebirth doctrine, Christian mysticism and Jewish mystical sources, making it one of the earliest testimonies of Christian Kabbalah. Wouter Hanegraaff, who edited Lazzarelli’s Hermetic writings with Ruud Bouthoorn in 2005, argues this is the purest example of a lived Christian Hermetic spirituality in the Renaissance: not antiquarian authority-citation in Ficino’s manner, but actual religious practice of the rebirth taught to Tat by Hermes. In esotericism studies this reframing — Lazzarelli, not Ficino or Pico, as “the real Hermetic tradition” — is now widely accepted. It has still not reached the general histories.

Why you’ve never heard of him

Popular occulture fixates on Ficino’s patronage story and Bruno’s pyre; Lazzarelli offers no execution, no Medici, no legend — only the rarer thing, a Renaissance man for whom the Hermetica were scripture to be practised rather than a pedigree to be cited. That gap in the record is exactly why he belongs here, mapped alongside the famous names on the Atlas.

Fair questions

Who was Lodovico Lazzarelli?

An Italian poet-humanist (1447–1500) who completed the Latin Corpus Hermeticum begun by Ficino and wrote the Crater Hermetis, the purest Christian-Hermetic text of the Renaissance.

What did Lazzarelli add to the Corpus Hermeticum?

The final three treatises (CH XVI–XVIII), translated around 1482 from a manuscript unknown to Ficino — completing the Latin corpus Renaissance Europe read.

Why is he called "the real Hermetic tradition"?

Because, per Wouter Hanegraaff, he actually practised Hermetic rebirth as a Christian spirituality — unlike Ficino and Pico, who mainly cited Hermes as an ancient authority.
